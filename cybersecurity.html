Cybersecurity

Learn to Defend the Digital World — Cybersecurity for Young Minds

Every system you use — your phone, your school, your bank — depends on people who know how to protect it. In this track, you'll learn how attackers think, how systems are defended, and how to build a real career in one of the world's most in-demand fields.

Join This Track Sponsor a Learner

Beginner-friendly  ·  8–10 weeks  ·  Ethical & legal-focused

What You'll Learn

From digital safety basics to ethical hacking

Four modules that take you from understanding the threats around you to building real defensive skills.

1. Cybersecurity Fundamentals

Understand how security really works — the CIA triad, common threats, and why every organisation needs people who understand them.

2. Network Security Essentials

Learn how the internet and networks work, and how to protect them — firewalls, protocols, monitoring and secure Wi-Fi.

3. Ethical Hacking & Penetration Testing

Learn how attackers think and how to safely test systems — in legal, controlled environments. The same skills professional pentesters use.

4. Digital Safety for Young People

Practical everyday skills: strong passwords, phishing awareness, privacy settings, and protecting your online identity.

Tools You'll Use

Learn on the same tools professionals use

All tools are used in safe, legal environments with proper guidance:

  • Linux — the foundation of security work
  • Wireshark — network traffic analysis
  • Nmap — network scanning fundamentals
  • Kali Linux — a safe security toolkit
  • TryHackMe & HackTheBox — practice environments

You care about online safety

Cyber FAQ

Common questions about this track

Is ethical hacking legal?

Yes — when done with permission, on systems you're authorised to test. We teach this distinction carefully and only practice in safe, legal environments.

Do I need advanced coding skills?

Not to start. Basic familiarity with computers is enough. We introduce scripting gently as you progress.

Do I need a powerful laptop?

A normal laptop is fine. We'll guide you on setting it up with the right tools. If hardware is a barrier, reach out.

What careers can this lead to?

Security analyst, penetration tester, SOC analyst, security engineer, and more. Cybersecurity has millions of unfilled roles globally.
